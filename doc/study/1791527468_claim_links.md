# Claim links: study

Date: 2026-10-09, Asia/Manila.

## Request

The human asked to start the study and plan for claim links: a host sends a link to a player who has no login, and that player's account takes over their existing place on the roster. It is the item left open in roadmap Stage 5 ([roadmap](../roadmap/README.md)), and it was left outside the password reset cycle and the roster management cycle ([plan](../plan/1791525658_roster_management.md)).

## What exists today

- A roster player without a login is a `Member` row with an empty `user`. A host acts for them. Results, seats, transfers and payments point at that row.
- An invite link (`Invite`, `groups/services.py`) puts an account into a group as a **new** `Member`. A regular who played ten sessions as "Tito Boy" and then signs up through the invite becomes a second person, "titoboy", with no history. Tito Boy's history stays on the row nobody can log in to.
- Nothing joins two member rows. The roster cycle only stopped a second row being created from a removed name.
- Since 2026-10-06 a player with a login records their own rebuy and sends their own final count (`ledger/services.py`, `member.user_id is not None`). A player without a login cannot.
- Every foreign key to `Member` is `PROTECT`: `Participant`, `PlayerResult`, `Transfer` (payer, payee) and `Payment` (payer, payee). A member row with none of these can be deleted; one with any cannot.
- `member_one_per_user_per_group` allows one member row per account per group, whatever the row's status.
- Two kinds of link already work the same way: a 32-byte random token, only its SHA-256 hash stored, an expiry, shown once with a Copy button (`Invite` in `groups`, `PasswordResetLink` in `accounts`).
- Sign-up on the public address needs an invite. `accounts/signup.py` holds three hook lists that `groups` fills: `CHECKS` (may this visitor sign up), `INVITERS` (which group to name on the entry pages) and `AFTER_SIGNUP` (finish what they came for, then say where to go). A newcomer from an invite link is in the group as soon as the account exists.
- Group settings → Players has a "Manage {name}" block per player (roster cycle), where the reset link is offered for members with a login.

## Constraints

1. **App direction** (AGENTS.md rule 11). A claim link ties an account to a member, so it belongs to `groups`. `groups` cannot ask `games`, `ledger` or `settlement` whether a member has records.
2. **Only services write, each write is audited** (rules 2 and 6). Group writes lock the group row, then the member row (the order set in the roster cycle).
3. **`request_id`** (rule 3). Group writes carry none. Creating a link cancels the earlier one, as a reset link does, so a double tap leaves one live link. Using a link is one-way and safe to repeat by the same account.
4. **Money records are append-only** (rule 4). A claim changes who can log in as a member. It moves no result, transfer or payment, and it rewrites none.
5. **A link is opened by more than its reader.** Chat apps fetch a pasted address for a preview. Opening the link must change nothing.
6. **The link is a credential.** Its holder becomes that player in the group: they see the group, and their own rebuys and counts are accepted. It must work once, expire, be stored as a hash and be cancellable.
7. **Partial unique constraints are not deferrable** ([footgun](../wiki/footguns/partial_unique_constraints_are_not_deferrable.md)). When an account that already has a row in the group claims another, the old row must let go of the account before the claimed row takes it, in that order, in one transaction.
8. **Missing locks pass on SQLite** ([footgun](../wiki/footguns/sqlite_hides_missing_locks.md)). Two people using one link at once needs a PostgreSQL test.
9. **No preview deployments.** The change must be additive with a switch that turns it off without a release.
10. **Entry pages** are built (DESIGN.md): the claim page uses that frame, and the host's side reuses the reset-link pattern and the Copy field. No new colour, font or asset.

## The cases a claim meets

The person who opens the link is one of these:

| Who | Their place in the group today |
|---|---|
| A. A newcomer | No account |
| B. An account that is not in the group | None |
| C. An account that joined by invite and never played | A second member row with nothing recorded |
| D. An account that joined by invite and has played | A second member row with seats, results or transfers |
| E. An account that was removed from the group | A removed row, empty or not |

C is the case that hurts today, and the one a host will most often want fixed: the regular already signed up the wrong way.

D needs two histories joined into one. A session where both rows sat at the table cannot be joined at all, and results, transfers and payments would have to be re-pointed, which is a change to money records. It needs its own study.

## The risk that shapes the design

A claim hands a person's whole record in the group to whoever opens the link first. If the host sends it to the wrong chat, a stranger becomes "Tito Boy": they see the group's sessions and results, and during a set they could record rebuys in Tito Boy's name.

What limits it: the host chooses who receives the link, it works once, it expires, the host can cancel it, and the host sees at once that the row now has a login. A host can undo the damage by removing the member, and every rebuy can be reversed. It gives no host power and reaches no other group. This is less than a reset link gives, and the same kind of trust as an invite.

What a claim cannot be is undone by the app: once an account holds the row, taking it off again would leave the person's own later entries attributed to a row with no login. So the page that uses the link must say plainly what is about to happen and name the account.

## Options

**Where the link lives**

- **A. A table in `groups` (recommended):** the member, the token's hash, expiry, used and cancelled times, who created it and which account used it.
- **B. Reuse `Invite` with an optional member.** One table, but an invite has a use count and a 20-use limit, and every query on invites would have to exclude claims.

**An account that already has a row in this group**

- **A. Replace an empty row, refuse a row with records (recommended).** If the account's own row has no seat, result, transfer or payment, that row is deleted and the claimed row takes the account, keeping the account's host role if it had one. If it has any record, the claim is refused and says why. Whether a row is empty is decided by the database: the delete is tried inside the transaction, and a `PROTECT` refusal means it has records. `groups` then needs no knowledge of the apps above it.
- **B. Refuse whenever the account is already in the group.** Simple, but it leaves case C, the common one, unsolved.
- **C. Join the two histories.** Out of reach for this cycle (see D above).

**After a newcomer signs up from a claim link**

- **A. Claim at once and open the group (recommended),** as an invite does. The account is new, so there is nothing to confirm.
- **B. Show the confirm page as well.** One more screen for a person who has just said who they are.

**An existing account**

- **A. One confirm page, then claim (recommended).** It names the group, the player, how many sessions come with it, and the account that will hold it. It also says what is replaced in case C.
- **B. Claim on opening.** Refused by constraint 5.

**How long a link lasts**

- **A. 7 days, one use (recommended).** It travels through a group chat like an invite, and unlike a reset link nobody is locked out while waiting.
- **B. 24 hours,** as a reset link.

**The player's name after the claim**

- **A. Keep the roster name (recommended).** The group knows them by it, and since the roster cycle they can change it themselves.
- **B. Change it to the username.** Loses the name everyone uses.

**Undoing a claim**

- **A. Not offered (recommended).** A wrong claim is handled by removing the member and adding the right person. The page and the wiki say so.
- **B. A host action "take the login off this player".** It would orphan whatever the account recorded afterwards.

## What stays outside this cycle

- Joining two member rows that both have records (case D), and any re-pointing of money records.
- Undoing a claim.
- A claim link for a member who already has a login.
- Claiming a removed player. The host brings them back first.
- A "change my password" screen, and sending any link by email or SMS.
