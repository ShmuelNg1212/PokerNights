# Table alerts in the header: study

Date: 2026-10-16, Asia/Manila. Base: `main` at `c5f6e49`.

## Request

"I want the option to turn on notifications to be a simple symbol on the top navbar instead of awkward button at the bottom of the your groups home page. This also shows a simple reminder to turn on notifications to the user who has not turned notifications on yet once per app session. use proper workflow, design, motion, and UI skills for this task."

Answered before this study (2026-10-16):

- **The bell's tap** opens a small panel under it: one sentence of reason and **Turn on**, or the "on" line with **Turn off**, or the "blocked" line. The phone's permission question comes only from Turn on.
- **iPhone in Safari** (push cannot work outside the Home Screen app): the bell still shows. Its panel says alerts work from the Home Screen app and opens the existing home-screen sheet. No reminder there.
- **The reminder** appears once per app launch, on Your groups only, pointing at the bell, after the arrival and the home-screen sheet. Never on a set page.

## Understanding

- **Said:** the table alerts control moves from the foot of Your groups to an icon in the header. A person whose alerts are off is reminded once per app session.
- **Assumed:** "app session" is one window or one launch of the installed app, which is what `sessionStorage` lasts for. The app already counts a launch this way (`pn-launched` in `base.html`). "Not yet turned on" means permission is still unasked and there is no subscription. A phone that refused (blocked) is not reminded, because only the phone's settings can change that.
- **Success:** the foot of Your groups no longer has the panel. On every signed-in page the header has a bell, and its state can be read at a glance. A player whose alerts are off sees the bell's panel open by itself, once, the first time they reach Your groups after opening the app. One tap turns alerts on.

## What happens now

- `templates/web/_alerts.html` is a panel at the foot of Your groups, after New group. It is included only when `push_alerts` is true and the person has a group (`web/views.py` `home`). It is `data-turbo-permanent` because the page redraws every 5 seconds.
- `static/js/alerts.js` shows it only where push can work: a service worker, `PushManager` and `Notification`, which an iPhone has only in the installed app. States: ask (with **Not now**, which hides it for 30 days through `localStorage` `pn-alerts-later`), on (with Turn off) and blocked. A phone that is on sends its subscription once per window (`sessionStorage` `pn-alerts-synced`).
- `push_alerts` and the public VAPID key reach only the home view. The header (`base.html`, `.site-header`) has the brand on the left and, on the right, the username and **Log out**. Login, signup, reset and closed-signup pages have no header; the invite and claim pages show it only when signed in.
- The home-screen sheet (`install.js`) opens once after a login on a phone's browser, 1100ms after the page shows when the arrival plays, 500ms otherwise. A page that opens its own dialog keeps the turn.
- The icon set (`table_tags.ICONS`, Lucide paths, 24px stroke) has no bell.

## Design direction (impeccable shape; animate)

Mode: **Operate**. Visual authority: the Rack in DESIGN.md. Nothing new is invented for one control: the header gains an icon button, the panel is the shared Rail card raised like a sheet, and the motion reuses existing presets.

### The bell

- A 48px icon button in the header, before the username. It has no visible label and an accessible name that includes the state: "Table alerts, off", "Table alerts, on", "Table alerts, blocked".
- The state shows in the glyph's shape, not in colour alone (DESIGN.md: never colour alone):
  - **Off, or to install:** an outline bell in Bone.
  - **On:** a filled bell in Bone.
  - **Blocked:** a slashed bell (bell-off) in Bone Dim.
- There is no badge or dot on the bell. The reminder does the asking, once. A permanent dot would ask on every screen.
- **Where push cannot work and installing would not help** (desktop Firefox in a private window, a browser without a service worker): no bell, as today.

### The panel

- It opens under the bell and is aligned to the header's right edge: `min(300px, 100vw − 32px)` wide, on Rail 2 with a 1px Line edge, the surface radius, and the sheet shadow, because it floats above the page. A small caret points at the bell.
- It is not modal. Focus moves to its first action. Escape, a tap outside it, or the bell again closes it, and focus returns to the bell. The bell carries `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls`.
- The content per state is today's panel, tightened:

| State | Panel |
|---|---|
| Ask | **Table alerts** · "Get a notification when a host adds you to a set." · neutral **Turn on** (48px). When the reminder opens it, a quiet **Not now** is added beside Turn on |
| On | A green tick, "On for this phone." and **Turn off** |
| Blocked | "Alerts are blocked for PokerNights in this phone's settings." |
| Install (iPhone tab) | **Table alerts** · "On an iPhone, alerts work from the Home Screen app." · neutral **Add to Home Screen**, which closes the panel and opens the existing home-screen sheet |
| Failure | The existing message, "That wasn't turned on. Check your connection and try again.", under the button, nudged |

### The reminder

The reminder is the same panel, opened by itself in the ask state with Not now. A second component would say the same thing in another place, and opening the real panel teaches where the control lives.

- **When:** on Your groups with at least one group, a visible tab, push possible, permission unasked, no subscription, and the reminder not yet shown in this window. It waits until the arrival is over (1100ms with the arrival, 600ms without). If the home-screen sheet or any other dialog is open or due on this page, it gives way and tries again on the next visit to Your groups in the same window.
- **Once:** `sessionStorage` `pn-alerts-reminded` is set when it opens. **Not now**, a tap outside and Escape all close it the same way. A storage failure means no reminder, never a repeat.
- **The 30-day Not now goes.** The request is once per app session. `alerts.js` removes the old `pn-alerts-later` key.

### Motion (animate skill gate)

| Moment | Frequency, purpose | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| The panel opens | Occasional; spatial (it comes from the bell) | Opacity with `fade` (180ms). The panel scales from 0.96 and drops 4px with `shift` (260ms spring, no bounce), from the top right at the caret |
| The panel closes | Occasional | `leave` (200ms), opacity and 4px up. Faster than opening |
| The reminder opens | Once per launch; explanation (where the control is) | The panel opens as above, and the bell swings once around its top: −14°, 10°, −6°, 0° over 600ms, with `nudge`'s ease-out. This is the only new keyframe set, within the 900ms budget for a once-only moment |
| Alerts turned on | Rare; state indication | The outline bell cross-fades to the filled one. The filled one comes up from 0.9 with `arrive`. The panel's lines cross-fade (`fade`) and the tick springs (`arrive`), as today |
| A failure | Rare; feedback | The message is nudged, as today |
| Reduced motion | — | No movement and no swing. The panel and the state changes appear at once |

Only `transform` and `opacity` animate. The first keyframe is written before Motion starts (footgun `motion_starts_a_frame_late.md`). Without Motion the panel simply shows and hides.

### Header at small widths

Brand (about 150px), bell (48), username and Log out (about 140) fit at 360px with 12px gaps. Below 360px the header already wraps. The plan hides the username there instead, so the bell and Log out stay on the brand's line. The username is not needed to act.

## Options considered

- **Where the bell's data comes from.** A. Add `push_alerts` and the public key to the `config.pwa.flags` context processor, so every page's header has them (recommended; `config` already reads settings and `notify.push.enabled()` is a leaf function). B. Pass them from each view (rejected; many views).
- **The panel's mechanics.** A. A button with `aria-expanded` and a hidden panel, about 40 lines in `alerts.js` (recommended; it works on every phone that can take push, including iOS 16.4). B. The native `popover` attribute (rejected for now; iOS 17 or later, and anchoring needs CSS that iOS lacks).
- **Turbo.** The header is redrawn with each page. The bell's wrapper keeps `id="alerts"` and `data-turbo-permanent`, so an open panel and its state survive the 5-second refresh of Your groups and every screen change.

## Costs and risks

- **No preview deployments** (the human tests on the live build). The change is UI only, with no migration and no server write. `PUSH_ALERTS=False` already hides the bell, the panel and the reminder, and leaves the app as it was before alerts. A separate switch to bring back the old foot panel would keep two versions of the same control. Recommended: none.
- **The header on every page** gains a script-driven control. Until `alerts.js` decides, the bell is `hidden`, so a page where push cannot work never shows a dead bell.
- **The reminder and the home-screen sheet** compete for the first moments after a login. The sheet keeps priority; the reminder waits for the next visit to Your groups in the same window.
- **iOS `sessionStorage`** in the installed app can outlive a quick switch away and back. That counts as the same app session, which matches the request.
- **The browser check `arrivals.mjs`** covers the old panel. Those checks move to a new `alerts_bell.mjs`.

## Conclusions

1. Remove the panel from the foot of Your groups. Put a bell icon button in the header of every signed-in page where push can work, or where installing would make it work (iPhone Safari).
2. The bell opens a small anchored panel with the states of today's panel, plus an install state for iPhone Safari that opens the existing home-screen sheet.
3. The reminder is that panel opening by itself, with Not now, once per window, on Your groups only, after the arrival and the home-screen sheet, for a phone whose permission is unasked. Remove the 30-day deferral.
4. Motion reuses `fade`, `shift`, `leave`, `arrive` and `nudge`. One new moment is added: a single swing of the bell when the reminder opens. Reduced motion removes all movement.
5. No new switch. No migration. No dependency.
